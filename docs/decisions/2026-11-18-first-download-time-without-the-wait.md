# The ops dashboard shows a first download's wait for a connection apart from its download time

Date: 2026-11-18 · By: Caleb Koster · Status: accepted

Reason: a phone's `open` record times a stream from its open to the first sync that reads it up to date (`recordOpen` in `packages/sync/src/syncClient.ts`), so its `ms` holds the time the phone waited with no connection and the download together. The ops dashboard named that number "a language's first download", and on 18 November 2026 one phone that opened a language with no connection for 21 minutes made the line "A language's first download took over 18 minutes", when its download took 0.2 seconds. The wait is real and stays in view, but it is not a slow download: a phone in the field is often offline, and a line about slow downloads sends people to the server. So the dashboard shows two numbers. The wait is the `offlineMs` of that phone's `sync` records of the stream during the open (`diag.open_wait_ms`); the download time is `ms` less the wait; the line `first-download-slow` (`fleetAlerts` in `apps/admin/src/sources/ops.ts`) reads the download time only and says counts ("2 of 8"). Only a cold open of a language is a first download (`diag.first_download`), counted once by its `open` record. The app records what it did before, and no record is changed or left out (migration `20261118234300_first_download_wait.sql`). Leaving the wait out of view was rejected (it hides how long a person stood before an empty language), and so was a change to the app's record (older builds in the field would keep the old shape for months, and the `sync` record already holds the wait).
Reverse if: the app starts to record the download time by itself, on every build the server still accepts, so the dashboard can read it and not take one record from another; or opens often lose their `sync` record on the way, so waits show again as download time.

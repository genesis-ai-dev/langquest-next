# An uploaded Bible is read as a Bible through one view (`sourceOf`), and one walk over the USFM serves the check and the reader

Date: 2026-11-17 · By: Caleb Koster · Status: accepted

Reason: Go Forth North India asked (LAN-230, meeting of 4 November 2026) to be sure LangQuest shows their USFM files as a real Bible. Two things had to be chosen.

How about 25 places that offer, list, count or read a `source@1` take a `usfmBible@1`. A second branch in each would drift, and copying an upload into `source@1` and `sourceBook@1` would undo decision `docs/decisions/2026-11-17-uploaded-usfm-bible-library-formats.md`. Core `sourceOf(doc)` gives whatever is a Bible as one shape, `BibleSource`: a `source@1` as it is, an upload as text in the library, kept on the phone, with no audio (`uploaded: true`). Nothing stored changes; the view is made on reading, one object per document so memos hold. `refKindOf` says `source`, so recommendations, My Bibles, More Bibles, coverage, Get ready and offline keeping all take it. Phones keep the books of the passages in their offline scope, as they do a library source's. A `usfmBible@1`'s dependencies load like a `source@1`'s: its numbering comes along and its books wait until a passage reads one (`docStore.ts`), or listing Bibles would fetch every book.

How the file is read. The first check read a file line by line, with `\v` found only at a line's start, and put a mid-verse `\s` and an alternate number (`\va`) into a verse's words; a split verse (`\v 4a`, `\v 4b`) was refused as out of order, and aligned text (`\zaln-s`) left raw markers. `usfmRead.ts` walks markers, not lines, and gives chapters of verses, each with its headings, lines (prose or poetry, how far in, a space before) and notes. `checkUsfm` now walks with it, so what was checked is what shows. Tests hold it on real books (Jonah in the Hindi IRV and the WEB, in `packages/core/test/fixtures/usfm`), the edge cases Aquila's USFM import got wrong (AQU-1465, AQU-1363, AQU-1400, AQU-1295), and any text at all: it never throws and no backslash reaches a word a person reads. Both whole Bibles were also run through it by hand, about 62,000 verses.

What a person sees. Verses stay rows with their numbers, as a Bible Brain text Bible's do, because a row is what takes a tap, a note, a key word and a highlight; a verse's poetry lines stack in its row, its headings stand above the row and its notes below. Paragraph breaks are a space before the row. Notes are always shown under their verse, with a letter in the text: a letter in a line is too small to tap (touch targets are 48pt), and a translator needs them. A split verse is one row (its verse is what a note and a passage name), each part keeping its number in the text. `row.text` stays plain words, so key words, word search and the record work unchanged. A Bible numbered otherwise maps through `mapRange` as any source does, and says so above the verses ("This Bible numbers these verses 2:1-2"), and says which of the passage's verses its file does not have: that is where the upload's missing numbering check (decision `docs/decisions/2026-11-17-uploaded-usfm-bible-library-formats.md`) reaches the person it matters to. A chapter the file lacks no longer hides the rest of a passage. More Bibles lists an upload with its file's book names and only the chapters it has, opens at the passage's chapter, marks the passage's verses and brings the first into view; this last part holds for every Bible there.
Reverse if: translators find the rows too broken up to read poetry or long paragraphs (then a reading view that runs verses on, as the passage's text bar already does, becomes the default), or an upload's notes crowd the reader (then a switch for them).
